The Giant Panda


Giant pandas (Ailuropoda melanoleuca) are iconic, beloved bears native exclusively to the high-altitude, mountainous regions of central China.


Diet: Despite belonging to the carnivore family, a panda's diet is 99% bamboo. Because bamboo is so low in nutrients, they must eat 26 to 84 pounds (12 to 38 kg) of it every single day, spending up to 14 hours a day just eating.


Unique Features: Their distinctive black-and-white coat helps them camouflage in snowy and rocky environments. They also possess a "pseudo-thumb"—an enlarged, flexible wrist bone that functions like a human thumb, allowing them to easily grip bamboo stalks.


Conservation Status: For decades, pandas were the global symbol for endangered species. Thanks to massive conservation, habitat protection, and breeding efforts, their status was officially upgraded from "Endangered" to "Vulnerable" by the IUCN in 2016. Today, there are roughly 1,860 giant pandas living in the wild.


Behavior: Pandas are solitary and peaceful animals. In the wild, they establish their own territories and generally avoid each other, only coming together during the brief spring mating season.


Fun Fact: Newborn pandas are extremely tiny! They are born blind, pink, and weigh only about 3 to 5 ounces (100 grams)—roughly the size of a stick of butter.
